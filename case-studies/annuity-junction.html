Annuity Junction

Designing a Zillow-style marketplace that helps clients find, evaluate, and book licensed financial professionals—all while supporting agent subscriptions and administrative oversight.

Solo Product Designer & DeveloperWordPressPre-launch

The problem

A client wanted a consolidated way for people to search for an agent by location, compare expertise, and decide who to trust before reaching out. I owned the project end to end: research, information architecture, interface design, and the technical build.

Three users

Clients

Primarily older adults who need to understand an agent’s location, specialty, and credibility before making contact.

Agents

Professionals paying for visibility who need credibility, profile control, content publishing, scheduling, and evidence of value.

Administrators

Staff overseeing agent content, memberships, payments, and platform quality.

Key decision

Search was not the hard part. Trust was.

A ZIP-code result can surface names, but it cannot help someone evaluate a high-stakes financial relationship. I built the profile system around multiple trust signals: detailed credentials, verified reviews, educational content, and clearly labeled featured placement.

This lets agents earn visibility through either paid positioning or demonstrated expertise, while giving clients meaningful context before they commit.

From found to booked

I integrated scheduling into the platform so clients can move directly from agent discovery to a confirmed appointment instead of being returned to a cold email process.

Technical obstacle

Major Elementor and WordPress changes altered core building patterns partway through the project. I front-loaded the learning time and rebuilt the affected components correctly instead of patching around the update. The project remained on schedule without creating fragile technical debt.

Outcome

The platform is onboarding early agent partners ahead of launch. The designed journey moves from location search to matched profiles, articles, trust signals, and booking. Agents receive profile, content, calendar, membership, and lead-management tools, while administrators retain oversight.

What this demonstrates: end-to-end product ownership, multi-role architecture, trust-centered conversion design, and delivery under real technical constraints.
