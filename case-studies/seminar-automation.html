Seminar lifecycle system

Replacing a manual, generic follow-up process with behavior-based communication and clear lifecycle visibility for approximately 12 educational events per year.

Sole Marketing & Systems DesignerHubSpotMicrosoft BookingsWordPress

The problem

The recurring seminar program began without marketing infrastructure. As the only marketing systems owner, I was responsible for the public-facing experience and the internal system tracking each attendee—with no prior workflow to build from.

Lifecycle design

The challenge was not simply collecting registrations. Every person could occupy a different state and needed different communication:

Registered or absent. Attended or no-show. Booked a follow-up or did not. Moved forward, needed time, or did not progress after an appointment.

I mapped these states into HubSpot branches connected to forms and Microsoft Bookings. Each meaningful action advances the contact into the correct communication path.

User-centered shift

My first pass focused on what information the company needed to collect. That was incomplete. Reframing the system around how a client decides whether to register, attend, meet, and move forward revealed more—and more specific—stages than the internal view alone.

Build versus buy

Zapier would have been faster to stand up, but it would not provide the lifecycle outcome visibility the team needed. I chose native HubSpot automation because measurement—not merely triggering actions—was central to the problem.

Outcome

The system replaced generic manual follow-up with communication based on actual behavior. The team can see where people are in the funnel and who needs attention, while the workflow runs without depending on one person maintaining it by hand.

What this demonstrates: multi-branch systems thinking, user-centered lifecycle design, deliberate technology tradeoffs, and ownership from website and forms through back-end automation.
